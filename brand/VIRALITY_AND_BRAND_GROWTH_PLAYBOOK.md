# NovaMatter — Audience Growth, Virality & Brand Integrity Playbook
**Established:** 2026-11-19 | **Audience:** prospective NovaLife and NovaLens users, sustainability-minded consumers, people building responsible technology, and B2B decision-makers.

## Non-negotiable mission
**New Matter. New Meaning.** Build a recognizable, credible platform at the intersection of technological intelligence, sustainability, and human flourishing. The work should feel clean, organic, optimistic, scientifically curious, flexible and technically ambitious. High energy is welcome; hype without proof is not.

This document is a permanent companion to [the Creative Standard](./CREATIVE_STANDARD.md). The established release gate is **36/40 or better, with no failures on brand identity, product demonstration, claims/privacy or CTA**. Every post still needs final human approval; marketing automation may research, draft and prepare but must not publish an unapproved asset. Respect the approved HeyGen credit budget, review limit and existing current-app artwork.

## Evidence-based principles (not an algorithm cheat code)
- **Viewer selection:** YouTube states Shorts are ranked by viewer relevance and performance; track the proportion who choose to watch rather than swipe away. A sensational but misleading hook is not a win.
- **Viewer experience:** Watch duration, average percentage viewed, likes and satisfaction matter. Shorter is not automatically better; deliver the payoff at the speed the story needs.
- **Meaningful differentiation:** Each video should convey a truly distinct insight, product interaction, experiment or story, not a template swapping nouns.
- **Native, yet premium:** Research by YouTube's Creative Works team (2,000+ Shorts ads) emphasizes feed-native, conversational storytelling rather than hard-sell advertising. Use premium cinematography selectively, but preserve human immediacy, real apps, and understandable motion.
- **Hook → substance → close:** TikTok's brand guidance recommends an immediate recognizable hook, product or idea in the middle, and one direct CTA; sound and captions should serve comprehension.
- **No posting-frequency myth:** YouTube says it has no required minimum Shorts cadence and publish time is not known to drive long-term performance. Aim for two differentiated, quality-checked posts per day **when assets and approval permit**; cancel low-value filler rather than manufacture it.
- **Monetization integrity:** YouTube's inauthentic-content policy excludes mass-produced, generic, repetitive or manipulative AI videos. Avoid reused music, copyright violations, fabricated testimonials, misleading health/environment claims, fake comments, purchased engagement and bot-generated views.

## Story worlds: one parent brand, three series
**The Evidence Layer / NovaLens:** What's hidden behind everyday purchases? Reveal a real confidence gap, product comparison, missing data label or lifecycle question. Avoid false certainty and never show unknown as zero.

**The Human Layer / NovaLife:** What makes everyday behavior more intentional? Use real Today, Track, Plan and reflection demonstrations. Show choice and agency without suggesting diagnosis, therapy or guaranteed life transformation. No depiction of user journals.

**New Matter Labs / NovaMatter:** Science, nature-inspired computing, sustainability engineering, intelligent infrastructure, founder observations and behind-the-scenes building. Each episode must make a technical idea legible to a curious person without a degree.

## Repeatable creative mechanisms that remain professional
- **The overlooked variable:** "Two products can look identical. One has evidence; one has a question mark." Show the labels and data source, not a fake score.
- **The three-second proof:** Show a real app action first; explain what it does only after the viewer sees the outcome.
- **A real-world question:** "What does a carbon number actually tell us?" Then show distinction between CO₂ and CO₂e and what is still unknown.
- **A 20-second decision:** Open with a choice, present one evidence variable, end with a modest, useful principle.
- **The researcher's notebook:** A founder or expert explains what a real product test taught the team, illustrated with its authentic evidence.
- **The quiet reveal:** A visually restrained one-shot product close-up or UI reveal, original sound design and a single on-screen sentence.
- **From natural system to engineered system:** Use sourced scientific reference footage/diagrams to explain a design hypothesis; clearly distinguish inspiration from functioning shipped technology.
- **The honest limitation:** Show a responsible "unknown" label, an interesting data ambiguity or what a product *cannot* conclude yet. Earn trust through accuracy, not a negative brand attack.

Rotate formats (documentary, editorial graphics, hands-on demo, founder POV, evidence-led explainer, premium cinematic) without losing the shared brand identity. 12–45 seconds for most discovery pieces is a planning range, *not* a magic ranking duration. Build weekly longer-form explainers when quality and resources allow; Shorts alone are not the entire business.

## The hindsight-proof / cringe veto (mandatory)
Reject a concept when any answer is **yes**:
- Would an informed viewer, environmental researcher or prospective enterprise partner find it childish or manipulative in six months?
- Does it rely on a shock claim, false urgency, fear-mongering, misleading before/after, invented metrics, pseudo-science or medical promises?
- Would it be incomprehensible without a trend sound, inside joke, emoji-heavy meme or viral dance?
- Does it copy a creator's character, editing pattern, thumbnail, specific premise or recognizable phrasing rather than transfer an abstract mechanism?
- Is the logo wrong, unapproved, absent when brand recognition matters, or an unnatural floating intro?
- Is the actual app missing from a product-focused video, or are app screens fabricated?
- Is the CTA unverified, mismatched to public build, or pointing to an under-construction website?
- Does the visual style substitute excessive zooms, glitches, sterile stock footage, hyperbolic voiceover or AI-generic nature composites for an actual idea?
- Is it a duplicate just to satisfy the second post of the day?
- Could publishing it weaken NovaLife wellness trust or NovaLens environmental credibility?

A humorous or playful piece may still pass when it is genuinely clever and specific to the product. The criterion is timeless quality, not forced seriousness.

## Publishing and research loop
**Morning discovery slot**
1. Verify the current social accounts and existing scheduled/approved posts; inspect own newest posts and replies.
2. Check platform-native creator guidance for material policy/ranking changes, not vague claims of a secret algorithm.
3. Research at most one bounded niche outlier lane and record independent creator count, date and limitations; consult remaining vidIQ credit budget.
4. Select one hypothesis, audience need and distinct concept; present hook, storyboard, exact feature evidence, accurate captions, music/license plan, official logo assets and verified CTA.
5. Apply the 36/40 quality rubric and cringe veto; create only under approved credit budget and human review. Save a draft; no automatic publishing.

**Evening learning slot**
1. Review available new evidence, watch performance and whether the morning concept is approved/published.
2. Develop one meaningfully distinct topic or format, or revise a weak draft rather than publishing filler.
3. For public posts, record actual URL, genuine views, first-second viewing/swipe rate when available, average view duration, completion, saves/shares, qualified channel subscribers, profile visits, app clicks/installs and sales/leads (if measurable).
4. Compare performance to same-channel posts at similar age and length. With little data, treat signal as exploratory; do not declare a formula proven.
5. Record one lesson and one new test for tomorrow. Distinguish creator analytics from speculation. No purchased engagement or artificial views.

**Weekly executive review**
- Rank authentic product-conversion outcomes above vanity impressions. Distinguish NovaLife installs/onboarding and NovaLens scans/qualified B2B demos from ad revenue.
- Review the last seven days: hook choice-to-watch, retention, shares/sends, branded search, linked installs, email opt-ins and any payout actually verified in Studio.
- A/B test only *one* meaningful variable per pair of otherwise comparable videos (hook wording, first visual, product insertion moment or CTA). Do not call tiny samples statistically significant.
- Rotate the creative treatments with strongest real engagement, without cloning the same video 14 times.
- Review brand risk, user feedback, video costs and monetization eligibility. Stop weak experiments; double down on validated stories.

## Monetization and financial controls
Connecting a payout account does **not** improve ranking or create ad revenue. Use **YouTube Studio → Earn** and the official AdSense for YouTube process after applicable eligibility and review. As of this writing YouTube states ads require 1,000 subscribers and either 4,000 qualified public long-form watch hours over 12 months or 10 million qualified Shorts views over 90 days, plus YPP requirements and approval. Beginning February 1, 2027, YouTube says continuing monthly Shorts revenue-sharing eligibility requires 10 million qualified Shorts views in the recent 90-day period; verify current rules at each later review. Shorts revenue involves eligible engaged views and accepted monetization modules; historical pre-acceptance views do not pay retroactively. Under-18 AdSense publishers require an eligible adult parent/guardian-managed account. Never request or store bank credentials in AI content or code, and do not route payment setup through social-media connectors.
